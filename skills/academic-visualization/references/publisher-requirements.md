# Publisher Requirements Orientation

Verified against official pages on 2026-08-05. Use this matrix to identify likely constraints, then check the target journal's current author guide and template before claiming compliance.

| Publisher or venue | Typical size | Finished text | Format and resolution highlights |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | 89 mm single column; 183 mm double column; maximum height 170 mm | 5–7 pt; standard editable fonts | Editable/vector-oriented main artwork where appropriate; embed fonts; accessible colors; minimize unused space |
| PLOS Medicine | 66.8–190.5 mm width; maximum height 222.3 mm | 8–12 pt in the general specification; its SigmaPlot recipe uses 8 pt | TIFF or EPS submission; 300–600 ppi; captions outside artwork |
| IEEE journals | 88.9 mm single column; 182 mm double column | Common template guidance: approximately 8–10 pt | Prefer PS/EPS/PDF vector; non-vector color/grayscale >300 dpi; black-and-white line art >600 dpi |
| Elsevier | Publisher examples: 90 mm single, 140 mm 1.5-column, 190 mm double | 7 pt normal and 6 pt sub/superscript are explicitly a rule of thumb, not a strict rule | Publisher examples: 300 dpi halftone, 500 dpi combination art, 1000 dpi line art; journal-specific guide overrides |
| Wiley | Broad guidance uses 80–180 mm and emphasizes final legibility | No single publisher-wide point-size rule in the general guide | PDF preferred for line art and 600 dpi when rasterized; PNG/TIFF and 300 dpi for images; journal-specific requirements may differ |

## What the matrix proves

- A fixed 7 pt minimum is not cross-journal: Nature uses 5–7 pt, PLOS specifies 8–12 pt, IEEE commonly uses 8–10 pt, and Elsevier calls 7 pt a rule of thumb.
- A fixed 300 DPI rule is not cross-journal: 300 dpi is common for photographs/halftones, while raster line art often requires 600–1000 dpi; vector artwork is resolution-independent.
- Roughly 89–90 mm is common for one-column figures in several print layouts, but PLOS and journal-specific templates differ. Width must be resolved from the actual venue.
- Accessibility, final-size legibility, semantic accuracy, efficient layout, and avoiding artificial upsampling are more general than any one font size, palette, legend placement, or canvas height.
